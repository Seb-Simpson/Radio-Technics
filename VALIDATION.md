# Validation: Radio Technics

Practical AI at Oundle. These are the ten validation questions for the Radio help hub.

**Labels:** **Known** means it comes from the station or the project. **Guess** means we haven't checked it yet. **To fill in** means only the people we interview can answer it. As the brief says, AI suggestions aren't evidence, so nothing here counts as evidence until we've spoken to real presenters.

## 1. Problem
When studio equipment stops working during a live show, presenters without technical knowledge don't know what's wrong or what's safe to do, and the station risks going silent on air. *(Known: from the project brief. How often it happens is a guess.)*

## 2. Person
**To fill in:** one named presenter at Mix 92.6 who has had kit go wrong mid-show (first name and role only). Not "all presenters".

## 3. Last time
**To fill in from that presenter:** when did it last happen, what broke, and what did they actually do? Write down what they *did*, not what they think they *would* do.

## 4. Today
**Guess (check in interviews):** they ring or message the station engineer, ask whoever else is in the studio, or work it out by trial and error. Some studios have a printed sheet of instructions. Ask whether this one does.

## 5. Why switch
**Guess:**
- It's built for *this* studio. It uses the station's own photos and shows where each piece of equipment is.
- It understands vague descriptions like "the green thing isn't working" and asks one simple question at a time.
- Silence on air is always handled first.
- It ends with a summary the presenter can pass to technical support, so the engineer gets the full picture straight away.

## 6. Competition
1. Calling or messaging the station engineer (the current fallback).
2. Broadcast Radio's own help pages for the Myriad playout software (general, not specific to this studio).
3. General AI chatbots such as ChatGPT, which don't know this studio's equipment and may invent instructions.

*(Guess: also ask whether the station has its own written guide.)*

## 7. Moat
If ChatGPT added this tomorrow, we would still have (**Known:** these already exist in the app):
- The station's own photographs, with the equipment marked on them.
- Equipment records specific to this studio. The station engineer still has to review them before they count as verified.
- A safety-first design: silence on air is escalated before anything else, the app never invents fixes, and only engineer-approved procedures can be shown.

The real moat is the station engineer's verified knowledge, which isn't public.

## 8. Money
- **Who uses it:** presenters, guests and volunteers. *(Known)*
- **Who pays:** the station. *(Guess)*
- **How much:** probably free or a small yearly fee for a community or small station. The value is avoiding dead air and fewer emergency calls to the engineer. *(Guess, to test)*

## 9. First five
Ask the station manager to introduce us to five presenters on the rota, and test with them in the studio between shows. Contact outside school goes through Jacobo. *(Plan)*

## 10. Riskiest assumption
**"Presenters will open and use the app mid-show when kit breaks, rather than just phoning the engineer."** If they won't, the idea fails.

**Test this week:** set up a pretend fault (for example, "the next song won't play"). Watch two staff use the live link without any help, and time how long it takes them to reach a useful answer or the support summary. Note where they get stuck. This matches the next step in the brief: nail the three most common faults and watch two staff use the app.
